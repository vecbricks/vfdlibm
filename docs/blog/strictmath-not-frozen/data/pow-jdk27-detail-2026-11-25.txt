# StrictMath.pow on JDK 27: where it changed, by how much, and what Spark returns

What was run and why: for the post "Even StrictMath is not frozen", how large JDK 27's changes to
StrictMath.pow are, whether they reach ordinary result sizes and a realistic expression, and
whether Spark's power() changes with them. A bit comparison, not a timing.

Date: 25 November 2026
Host: laptop, AMD Ryzen AI 9 HX PRO 370, Linux 7.0.0-44-generic
JDKs: 25.0.5.1+2-1-26.04.1-Ubuntu and 27.0.1.1+4-22 (jdk.java.net)
Spark: Apache Spark 4.3.0 (git revision 98bc0212c94; see cents-fdlibm-vs-glibc-2026-11-25.txt),
      spark-sql --master 'local[1]' -S -f power-jdk.sql, with JAVA_HOME at each JDK
Commands:
      java Moderate.java > mod-<jdk>.txt     (on each JDK; the lines whose result differs are
                                              pow-moderate-jdk25-vs-jdk27.txt, as "x y jdk25 jdk27")

      python3 errors.py data/pow-strictmath-jdk25-vs-jdk27.txt
      python3 errors.py data/pow-moderate-jdk25-vs-jdk27.txt

## Reading

- On the inputs where JDK 27 changed StrictMath.pow with a normal result in JdkScan's aimed set,
  JDK 25's result was more than 1 ulp off on every one, up to 653 ulps, and JDK 27's is within
  0.74 ulp: the fix of JDK-8326547 does what its title says.
- At ordinary result sizes the change is rarer, 17 of 4,000,000 inputs here, and both results
  are within 1 ulp; JDK 27's is the nearer on 5 and JDK 25's on 12. There the change moves bits
  without an error to fix.
- Per-second compounding over 70 to 300 years reaches the changed branch but changed none of its
  462,000 results here.
- Spark 4.3.0's power() follows the JVM it runs on: the same query returns another double on
  JDK 27 than on JDK 25. (Spark ran on JDK 27 for this; whether Spark supports JDK 27 is not
  claimed.)
