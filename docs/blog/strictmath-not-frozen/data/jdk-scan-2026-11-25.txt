# StrictMath on JDK 25, 26 and 27, and vfdlibm 0.3.0 against each

What was run and why: for the post "Even StrictMath is not frozen" and issue #175, whether a JDK
after 25 changes StrictMath's bits for any of vfdlibm's 13 functions, and whether vfdlibm 0.3.0
still equals StrictMath there. A bit comparison, not a timing. StrictMath's and vfdlibm's
counts here do not depend on the CPU; the Math.pow rows do (the JIT's intrinsics are chosen per
CPU), so they are this x86-64 laptop's only.

Date: 25 November 2026
Host: laptop, AMD Ryzen AI 9 HX PRO 370 (flags: avx2 avx512f avx512dq), Linux 7.0.0-44-generic
JDKs: 25.0.5.1+2-1-26.04.1-Ubuntu, 26.0.2+10-2-26.04.2-Ubuntu (both Ubuntu 26.04 packages),
      27.0.1.1+4-22 (jdk.java.net)
vfdlibm: io.github.vecbricks:vfdlibm:0.3.0 from Maven Central, sha256
      4a675dbb2c38ade1c266941b9d8dfe7dd7245381ad8367d62059040b420617d3 (equal to Central's .sha256)
Inputs: JdkScan.java at the commit of this file: vfdlibm's hard cases (src/test/resources/.../
      hard-cases at 0f455af) plus 3,000,000 seeded random inputs per function, and for pow
      1,000,000 more aimed at fdlibm's branch for |y| > 2^31 with |x| near 1 (500,262 of them
      with |x| within 2^-20 of 1, of which 389,513 reach that branch's arithmetic; the others
      have a negative x with a non-integer y, which returns NaN first: aimed.py). On a lane of two NaN arguments the .bin files and digests
      hold 0, since StrictMath's NaN there depends on the JIT tier.
Commands, per JDK, with and (25, 26) without the libm intrinsics
      (-XX:+UnlockDiagnosticVMOptions -XX:-UseLibmIntrinsic):
      javac --add-modules jdk.incubator.vector -cp vfdlibm-0.3.0.jar -d . JdkScan.java   (JDK 25)
      java -Xmx3g [flags] --add-modules jdk.incubator.vector -cp .:vfdlibm-0.3.0.jar JdkScan <hard-cases> out-<jdk>
then:
      python3 compare.py out-25 out-26
      python3 compare.py out-25 out-27 --witnesses data/pow-strictmath-jdk25-vs-jdk27.txt
      python3 compare.py out-25-nolibm out-26-nolibm --file pow-Math.bin
      python3 compare.py out-25 out-26 --file pow-Math.bin
      python3 compare.py out-25 out-27 --file pow-Math.bin
      python3 compare.py out-25 out-25-nolibm --file pow.bin --after-file pow-Math.bin
      python3 compare.py out-26-nolibm out-27 --file pow-Math.bin --after-file pow.bin
      python3 witness_check.py out-25/pow.bin out-27/pow.bin out-26-nolibm/pow-Math.bin out-27/pow.bin
      python3 witness_check.py out-25/pow.bin out-27/pow.bin out-25-nolibm/pow-Math.bin out-25/pow.bin
      python3 aimed.py out-25/pow-inputs.bin

## Reading

- StrictMath gives the same bits on JDK 25, 26 and 27 for 12 of the 13 functions on every input
  here. StrictMath.pow is the same on 25 and 26 and differs on 27 on 1,258 of 4,005,016 inputs,
  all in the aimed set (listed in pow-strictmath-jdk25-vs-jdk27.txt as "x y jdk25 jdk27").
- JDK 26.0.2 changes Math.pow, not StrictMath.pow: with the libm intrinsics off, Math.pow on
  26 differs from 25 on the same 1,258 inputs and returns there the same bits as StrictMath.pow
  on 27. Elsewhere that Math.pow equals StrictMath.pow but for the bits of a NaN result, on JDK
  25 as on 26. With the intrinsics on, as by default on x86, Math.pow does not run that code.
- vfdlibm 0.3.0 differs from StrictMath on no lane, at 256 and 512 bits, on 25, 26 and 27 (a
  lane of two NaN arguments checked against the contract's two candidates): its pow finishes the
  lanes with |y| > 2^31 per lane by StrictMath.pow (Pow.java), and those are the lanes JDK 27
  changed. 0.3.x supports JDK 25 only; this does not change that.
